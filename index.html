What is diffuse midline glioma

This is not medical advice, and decisions about a particular child belong with that child's own care team.

The disease

Diffuse midline glioma, often shortened to DMG, is a tumour that grows in the middle structures of the brain and spinal cord: most often the pons, which is part of the brainstem, and also the thalamus and the spinal cord. When it arises in the pons it is also called diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma, or DIPG. The word diffuse matters. The tumour cells spread between normal brain cells rather than forming a lump with a clean edge, which is why surgery to remove it is not possible in the way it often is for other tumours.

Most of these tumours carry a single change in a protein that packages DNA, a histone. The change is called H3 K27M, and the current classification names the disease after it: diffuse midline glioma, H3 K27-altered. That one change alters how thousands of genes are switched on and off, which is why the disease behaves so differently from adult brain tumours.

Who it affects, and what the numbers say

DMG is mainly a disease of children, although it also occurs in teenagers and adults. In the most complete population based analysis, covering every brainstem high grade glioma recorded in the United States between 2000 and 2017, incidence was highest in children aged one to nine years and lower in older children (Patil et al., 2021). It is a rare disease, and because many of these tumours are diagnosed on imaging rather than biopsy, published counts vary with how cases are defined; the registry figures above are the ones to work from rather than a single global number. In the largest registry series, covering 1,008 children with diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma, median survival was 11 months from diagnosis and about one in ten children were alive at two years (Hoffman et al., 2018). Those numbers describe a group, not an individual, and they predate the treatments now in trials.

How it is diagnosed and treated today

Diagnosis usually begins with an MRI, which in a typical pontine tumour is characteristic enough that the picture alone can make the diagnosis. Many centres now also take a small biopsy, which carries risk but allows the tumour's molecular type to be established and makes trial entry possible. Radiotherapy is the one treatment with a reliable effect on survival, usually given over about six weeks, and it often brings a period of improvement in symptoms. Chemotherapy as used in other childhood cancers has not been shown to help.

In August 2025 the US Food and Drug Administration granted accelerated approval to dordaviprone, also known as ONC201, for diffuse midline glioma with an H3 K27M mutation and progressive disease after prior therapy, in adults and children aged one year and older (FDA announcement). Accelerated approval means the drug was approved on tumour response rather than on proven survival benefit, and a confirmatory trial is under way (Arrillaga-Romany et al., 2024). Early trials of CAR T cells, immune cells engineered to recognise the tumour, have produced measurable tumour shrinkage in some patients, with serious side effects that required intensive care (Monje et al., Vitanza et al.). These are early phase studies, which means they are testing safety and signal, not proving benefit.

Why it is hard, and what is changing

Three things make DMG difficult. Its location rules out surgical removal. The blood brain barrier keeps many drugs out of the brain, which is why trials now test delivery directly into the tumour or through focused ultrasound that opens the barrier temporarily. And the tumour is driven by a change in how genes are regulated rather than by a single faulty protein that a drug can switch off.

What has changed in the last few years is measurement. Tumour DNA can now be detected in cerebrospinal fluid, so response can be tracked without repeated surgery (Nobre et al., 2026). Tumours can be described at the level of proteins and their modifications, not only genes. And the research community has agreed a shared road map for how trials and data sharing should work (Koschmann et al.).

Where to find help

For families, the most useful first contacts are the treating centre's neuro-oncology team and the family support services run by the disease foundations, which can explain options and trials in person. My DIPG Navigator at the ChadTough Defeat DMG Foundation offers one to one support, and dipg.org keeps plain language summaries of current research. The trial table on this page lists studies that are open, but eligibility is decided by the trial team and a referral normally comes through the treating oncologist.

Named DMG datasets

  • CPTAC pediatric brain tumor proteogenomicsProteome and phosphoproteome with matched genome and transcriptome for 218 pediatric brain tumours, 25 of them high grade glioma. Processed data are open through the Proteomic Data Commons.
  • OpenPBTA and the CBTN cohortHarmonized genome and transcriptome for 1,074 pediatric brain tumours including H3 K27M altered DMG, analysable in the cloud through the Kids First portal.
  • EGAS00001005773, H3 K27M single cell and epigenomic cohortThe controlled access study behind Jessa et al., Nature Genetics 2022, on cell lineages in midline glioma. Access through an EGA data access committee.
  • CNS tumour methylation reference cohortThe reference set behind DNA methylation classification, including the DMG K27 class. The classifier runs online; reference data are requested from the authors.
  • DepMap and the pediatric dependency mapCRISPR and RNAi dependency data across cancer cell lines, with a pediatric panel of 82 lines. H3 K27M primary cultures are poorly represented, which is worth knowing before using it for DMG.

Specimens, catalogues and browsers

  • Children's Brain Tumor Network (CBTN)Specimens, clinical annotation and harmonized sequencing from member institutions; specimen and data requests go through the CBTN platform.
  • PedcBioPortalBrowser for pediatric cancer genomics, including the OpenPBTA and CBTN cohorts, for looking without downloading.
  • Childhood Cancer Data CatalogIndex of childhood cancer datasets and the access route for each, wider than brain tumours.
  • Human Protein AtlasTissue, subcellular and single cell type protein localization in normal tissue, used as a reference rather than as tumour data.

Search the archives for DMG data
